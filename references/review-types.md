# Review Type Guidance

Use this file only when review type affects the expected transparency standard.

## Narrative review

Typical features:

- broad or expert-led literature selection;
- no claim of exhaustive retrieval;
- interpretive or conceptual synthesis;
- may not provide a fully reproducible search protocol.

Expected minimum:

- clear scope and purpose;
- transparent explanation of how literature was identified;
- acknowledgement of selection limitations;
- evidence that major competing perspectives were not ignored;
- claims proportionate to the non-exhaustive design.

Do not require a PRISMA flow diagram by default.

## Structured critical review

Typical features:

- explicit databases or search themes;
- defined core studies;
- inclusion/exclusion logic;
- tables, coding, or cross-study comparison;
- may adapt systematic-review tools without claiming a full systematic review.

Expected minimum:

- reproducible-enough search description for the central corpus;
- stable inclusion boundary;
- traceable counts when `n/N` or percentages are reported;
- explicit handling of borderline studies;
- synthesis method clear enough for another researcher to understand how conclusions were produced.

## Systematic-style review

Use this label when the manuscript behaves like a systematic review but does not fully meet or claim a formal systematic-review standard.

Typical features:

- explicit database search;
- structured screening;
- defined final sample;
- numerical reporting of included studies;
- formal synthesis or coding.

Expected minimum:

- complete search strings or equivalent reproducible strategy;
- search dates;
- database-by-database record counts;
- deduplication and screening path;
- full-text inclusion/exclusion logic;
- clear coding method;
- denominator traceability.

## Systematic review

Apply formal reporting standards appropriate to the field and journal.

PRISMA may be relevant, but do not assume every engineering systematic review requires every medical-review convention.

Check whether the manuscript's own claimed standard has actually been followed.

## Scoping review

Focus on:

- breadth of evidence mapping;
- transparent scope and selection;
- classification of evidence types;
- gap mapping;
- avoiding conclusions stronger than a scoping design supports.

Do not expect a conventional effect synthesis unless it is explicitly attempted.

## Methodology review

Focus on:

- comparison of methods under compatible conditions;
- measurement and boundary-condition definitions;
- assumptions;
- transferability;
- validation status;
- strengths and failure modes of methods;
- whether recommendations are operational.

Methodology reviews often need more engineering comparability analysis than generic review checklists provide.

## Framework / taxonomy review

Focus on whether the proposed classification system can be reused.

Require:

- explicit criteria;
- examples;
- borderline handling;
- evidence mapping;
- demonstration that the framework improves interpretation or decision-making.

## Mixed review types

A manuscript may combine multiple forms.

Example:

- structured search + critical methodology review + framework proposal.

Apply the relevant checks to each component rather than forcing one label.

## Core rule

Transparency requirements should follow the **strength of the manuscript's claims**.

The more the paper claims that its literature set is complete, representative, quantitatively coded, or reproducible, the more transparent the retrieval and screening process must be.
